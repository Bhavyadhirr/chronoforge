MARK-B.L.U.

2025 · Quantum Hashing Mechanism for "SHAPESHIFTING" AI Identities


The Beginning

The work started off as simply Mark-1, when I first began working in and studying quantum hashing primitives and its applicative regions for blockchain. Continuing it independently for the next 6 months, I established the quantum-basis of the hashing mechanism. Subsequent to this, while working on the idea with Harshvardhan, the Chief Technology Officer at GENORROW, who added the badge rotation mechanism, it led into the built of the MARK-B.L.U. 1.0, which was made to be a quantum-hybrid cryptographic framework fundamentally designed to explore entropy amplification, collision resistance, and identity binding using parameterized quantum circuits on NISQ-era devices, to be used for verification and security of AI identities.


The Architecture

The system leverages quantum state evolution and measurement-induced randomness as a core primitive rather than treating quantum randomness as an auxiliary source. Classical inputs are encoded into quantum states through structured rotation and entanglement layers, followed by controlled interference and multi-basis measurement. The resulting bitstrings are post-processed to form fixed-length cryptographic hashes.


Unlike the conventional hash functions, MARK-B.L.U. highly emphasizes entropy provenance, explicitly tracking how randomness emerges from circuit depth, gate choice, and measurement statistics. The framework includes empirical analysis of avalanche behavior, collision resistance, output bias, and bit-independence across varying circuit configurations.


The architecture is intentionally modular, allowing experimentation across circuit topologies, encoding strategies, measurement bases, and noise-aware entropy extraction.

It was built to serve both as a research platform for quantum-assisted cryptography and a conceptual bridge toward post-classical security mechanisms for AI and autonomous infrastructure.
